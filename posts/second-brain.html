A Second Brain That Actually Gets Used

21 Sep 2026 · 2 min read

I've started and abandoned more note-taking systems than I can count. Folders inside folders, tags nobody used, templates prettier than the notes in them. They all died the same way: putting things in was easy, getting them back out wasn't.

The version that finally stuck is boring. One Obsidian vault, plain Markdown files, and a few habits.

Write for future me

A note is useless if I can't understand it in six months. So every note starts with one or two sentences answering "what is this and why did I save it?" before any details.

Explain, don't copy

Copying a paragraph from a book feels productive and teaches nothing. Rewriting it in my own words, with my own example, is slower and much more useful. If there's math, I derive it step by step until it feels obvious.

Link more than you file

I barely use folders. Notes link to each other, and the links do the organizing. A note on database indexes links to the one on B-trees, which links to a bug I fixed at work. That chain is where the real understanding lives.

A second brain isn't storage. It's a conversation with yourself that keeps going.

Some of the posts here started as notes in that vault. That might be the best test of a note system: whether anything ever grows out of it.
